Filtered Dynamic Inversion for Constrained Uncertain Systems

Integrating Filtered Dynamic Inversion with Model Predictive Control for disturbance rejection and actuator-constraint enforcement under limited plant knowledge.

Overview

This project develops a hybrid Filtered Dynamic Inversion–Model Predictive Control (FDI–MPC) framework for uncertain linear systems subjected to unknown and unmeasured disturbances and actuator constraints.

Conventional Dynamic Inversion (DI) can provide a convenient linearized input-output representation, but requires detailed knowledge of the plant, state information, and disturbance terms. Filtered Dynamic Inversion (FDI) substantially reduces these requirements and provides robust disturbance rejection; however, it does not inherently prevent actuator saturation. MPC provides the missing constraint-handling capability.

Main idea: use FDI for robust control with limited model knowledge, MPC to select the virtual control input, and a new constraint-mapping algorithm to guarantee that the actual filtered actuator command remains within its prescribed limits.

Control Problem

Consider an uncertain MIMO linear system subjected to an unmeasured disturbance:

\[ \dot{x}=Ax+Bu+w, \qquad y=Cx+Du, \]

with actuator constraints

\[ u_{\min}\leq u(t)\leq u_{\max}. \]

The objective is to make the output \(y\) follow a desired reference while rejecting disturbances and respecting the actuator limits. The plant matrices, states, initial condition, and disturbance are not assumed to be available to the controller. Instead, the required plant information is limited to the system's relative degree and first nonzero Markov parameter.

Filtered Dynamic Inversion

For a system with relative degree \(d\), ideal Dynamic Inversion constructs a control input that produces the desired virtual input \(\nu\). Its direct implementation, however, depends on unavailable model, state, and disturbance information.

FDI overcomes this limitation by passing the ideal inversion command through a stable high-gain low-pass filter. The resulting implementable control law can be written as

\[ \left[\eta_z(p)-\eta_z(0)\right]u = \eta_z(0)H_d^{-1} \left(\nu-p^d y\right), \]

where \(H_d\) is the first nonzero Markov parameter and \(\eta_z(p)\) defines the FDI filter. Consequently, the controller requires only \(d\), \(H_d\), and the measured output and its history rather than the complete plant model.

FDI–MPC Architecture

For sufficiently large filter gain, the FDI-controlled plant approximately behaves as a chain of integrators:

\[ p^d y \approx \nu. \]

This simple input-output representation is used as the prediction model for MPC. Rather than directly computing the physical actuator command, MPC determines the virtual input \(\nu\) that minimizes tracking error and control effort.

Dynamic Constraint Mapping

A central challenge is that MPC does not directly command the actuator. The MPC output \(\nu\) first passes through the FDI filter before producing the physical input \(u\). Therefore, simply imposing the actuator limits directly on \(\nu\) does not guarantee that \(u\) will remain feasible.

The proposed method constructs a state-space representation of the known filter dynamics and dynamically maps the actuator constraints into corresponding bounds on the MPC virtual input:

\[ \nu_{\min}(k) \leq \nu(k) \leq \nu_{\max}(k). \]

These bounds vary with the filter state, the history of the applied input, and the measured output derivative. As a result, MPC can optimize \(\nu\) while ensuring that the actual filtered command satisfies \(u_{\min}\leq u\leq u_{\max}\).

Important property: the constraint mapping is derived from the fully known FDI filter dynamics rather than the uncertain plant model. Its validity therefore does not depend on having an accurate model of the plant.

Three-Mass Case Study

The framework is evaluated on a serially connected three-mass–spring–damper system. The control force is applied to the first mass, disturbances can act on the individual masses, and the position of the third mass is required to track a reference trajectory.

The system has relative degree \(d=4\) and first nonzero Markov parameter \(H_d=40\). The simulations examine command following, rejection of unknown disturbances, actuator saturation, and the effect of the FDI filter gain.

Disturbance Rejection & Constraint Handling

Under unknown white-noise disturbances, increasing the FDI filter gain improves tracking and brings the filtered controller closer to ideal Dynamic Inversion behavior.

When actuator constraints are introduced, the mapped virtual-input bounds allow MPC to prevent saturation. As the physical limits become tighter, tracking performance decreases because less control authority is available, while the commanded input remains inside the prescribed bounds.

Relation to Ideal Dynamic Inversion

As the FDI filter gain increases, the FDI virtual-input constraint bounds approach those obtained from ideal DI. At the instants when the physical actuator reaches its upper or lower limit, the corresponding FDI and DI mapped bounds coincide.

The approximation quality is also evaluated using the discrepancy between \(p^d y\) and \(\nu\). Higher filter gains initially improve the linearization accuracy, although excessively large gains can introduce discrete-time oscillations and instability. This exposes a practical trade-off between filter gain, sampling rate, performance, and computational requirements.

Comparison with DI–MPC + Disturbance Observer

The proposed controller is further compared with a conventional DI–MPC architecture equipped with a Luenberger disturbance observer. The observer-based approach explicitly estimates the unknown disturbance before compensating for it through Dynamic Inversion.

This comparison highlights a central distinction between the two approaches: DI–MPC with disturbance estimation relies on knowledge of the plant matrices and state information, whereas FDI–MPC achieves disturbance rejection using substantially less plant information and without explicitly reconstructing the disturbance.

Key Contributions & Takeaways

  • A hybrid FDI–MPC framework combining robust disturbance rejection with explicit actuator-constraint handling.
  • A dynamic constraint-mapping algorithm that transforms physical actuator limits into feasible bounds on the MPC virtual input.
  • Control with limited plant information: only the relative degree, first nonzero Markov parameter, and output measurements are required by the FDI formulation.
  • Rejection of unknown and unmeasured disturbances without requiring explicit disturbance estimation.
  • Analysis of the practical relationship between FDI gain, sampling time, linearization accuracy, and ideal Dynamic Inversion behavior.
